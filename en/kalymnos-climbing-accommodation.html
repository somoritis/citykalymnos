Climbing in Kalymnos — and where to stay

More than 4,500 sport routes on some of the best limestone in the world. Here is what the climbing is like, when to come, and an honest answer to the question every climber asks: Masouri or Pothia?

Kalymnos is one of the world's great sport climbing destinations. The rock is limestone of exceptional quality — slabs, vertical walls with pockets, and hugely overhanging caves covered in tufas and stalactites. It is strictly a sport climbing venue: no bouldering, no trad, but several excellent bolted multi-pitches on Kalymnos and neighbouring Telendos.

The rock and the routes

The rock and the routes

There are over 4,500 routes across dozens of sectors, graded from 3a to 9a on the French scale. Roughly half of them are 6b+ or easier, and more than a third sit between 6a and 6b+ — which is why Kalymnos suits new climbers and people moving from the gym to real rock just as well as it suits the strong.

At the other end, there are more than 700 routes graded 7b and above. The hardest confirmed line on the island is 9a (Los Revolucionarios, sector Odyssey), first climbed by Adam Ondra in 2009.

Most routes are around 30 m long. Bring at least 15 quickdraws and a 70 m rope — an 80 m rope is a better idea, since many newer lines run 40–45 m. For the big cave routes at Grande Grotta and Sikati you will also want 5–6 long slings to keep the rope off the tufas. Always tie a knot in the end of your rope.

When to come

When to come

Autumn is the season. September and October are usually dry, and mid-October to late November is close to perfect. October is peak — expect the crags and the villages to be busy.

Spring is almost as good. There is more chance of rain and the tufas can seep after a wet winter, but Kalymnian rock dries fast. Winter brings plenty of mild, sunny days and the sun-facing crags between Arginonta and Emporios come into their own.

Summer is better than people expect, as long as you follow the rule: climb in the shade. Sectors such as Secret Garden and Arginonta Valley stay shaded most of the day and catch a breeze.

The sectors

The famous ones are Grande Grotta and Sikati Cave — vast overhanging caves where you climb on stalactites. Arhi, Odyssey, Spartan Wall and Poets are among the oldest and best-known.

For moderate grades, Panorama, Kasteli, Secret Garden, Arginonta Valley, Spartacus and Jurassic Park all repay a visit. Most sectors sit above the west-coast villages of Masouri, Armeos and Arginonta.

Masouri or Pothia? An honest answer

Most climbers stay in Masouri or Armeos, directly beneath the crags. If your priority is walking to the rock in 15–30 minutes without a vehicle, that is the right choice, and we will say so.

Pothia is a different kind of stay, and it suits some climbers better than others. Pothia is the island's real town and its port. It is 9 km from Masouri — a 20–30 minute drive on a winding coast road.

Pothia works well if…

  • you arrive by ferry and want zero transfer on arrival day — the boat docks a few minutes' walk away
  • you are renting a scooter or car anyway (rentals are right at the port)
  • you want a town, not a strip of climbing bars — bakeries, tavernas, a supermarket, pharmacies and the fish market are all within 30–100 m
  • you are travelling with family or a non-climbing partner who would rather have a town than a crag
  • you are here off-season, when much of Masouri is shut and Pothia stays open all year
  • you want a full kitchen and a washing machine after a week of climbing

Masouri is the better call if…

  • you want to walk to the crag every day with no vehicle
  • you want to be surrounded by other climbers in the evenings
  • you are here for a short, intense trip and every minute counts

Getting to the crags from Pothia

Getting to the crags from Pothia

Scooter or car is what most people do — 20–30 minutes to Masouri, and rentals are available right at the port in Pothia. Buses run along the west coast but only a few times a day, so check the timetable before you rely on one. A taxi to Masouri costs roughly €16–20.

Safety on the island

Kalymnos is a marine environment and corrosion is a real problem. Since 2025 the priority has shifted from bolting new routes to rebolting existing ones with titanium glue-in bolts. Check your hardware, and report anything suspect.

There is no professional mountain rescue service and no helicopter rescue on the island. In an emergency call 112. Hospital: +30 22433 61931. Climb with judgment.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kalymnos good for beginners?

Yes. About half of the more than 4,500 routes are graded 6b+ or easier, and over a third are between 6a and 6b+. The bolting is close and consistent, which is why Kalymnos is often recommended for a first outdoor trip.

When is the best time to climb in Kalymnos?

Autumn is the best season — September and October are usually dry, and mid-October to late November is close to ideal. Spring is almost as good. Winter has many mild sunny days, and summer works if you stick to shaded sectors such as Secret Garden and Arginonta Valley.

How far is Pothia from the climbing sectors?

Masouri, where most of the sectors are, is 9 km from Pothia — a 20 to 30 minute drive. Scooters and cars can be rented at the port. Buses run a few times a day and a taxi costs roughly 16 to 20 euros.

Should I stay in Masouri or Pothia?

If you want to walk to the crag without a vehicle, Masouri is the better choice. Pothia suits you if you arrive by ferry and want no transfer, are renting a scooter anyway, want a real town with shops and tavernas, are travelling with non-climbers, or are visiting off-season when much of Masouri is closed.

What gear do I need for Kalymnos?

At minimum 15 quickdraws and a 70 m rope. An 80 m rope is safer because many newer routes are 40 to 45 m. For the cave routes at Grande Grotta and Sikati bring 5 to 6 long slings to reduce rope drag. Always tie a knot in the end of your rope.

Is there bouldering or trad climbing on Kalymnos?

No. Kalymnos is strictly a sport climbing venue. There are, however, several good bolted multi-pitch routes on Kalymnos and on neighbouring Telendos.

Can I climb in Kalymnos in summer?

Yes, if you climb in the shade. Sectors like Secret Garden and Arginonta Valley are shaded almost all day and often catch a breeze. Start early and avoid the sun-facing crags.
